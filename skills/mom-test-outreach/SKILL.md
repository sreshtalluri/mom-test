# Mom Test — Outreach

Write the message the user needs. Keep every message under 120 words. Never link to or
describe the product. Never ask for "feedback on my idea".

## Cold outreach — the five-part framing

1. **Vision** — "I'm working on making [specific pain] less painful for [specific role]."
2. **Framing** — "I'm not selling anything. I'm early and trying to understand this
   before building the wrong thing."
3. **Weakness** — one genuine, specific unknown. Not "I'd love to learn more" but "I can't
   tell whether the pain is in building the schedule or reacting when it breaks at 6am."
4. **Pedestal** — one specific, true credential of theirs. "You've run ops at two
   carriers." "Your post about X." Not generic flattery.
5. **Ask** — small and concrete. "15 minutes to hear how it actually works at [company]?"

Subject line: about *their* thing, not yours. "how you handle driver scheduling", not
"quick question" or "startup idea".

If the user hasn't given you a real weakness or a real credential, ask for those two
things in one line before writing. They're what make the message work.

## Warm intro (send to the introducer, ready to forward)

Two lines for the introducer, then a forwardable paragraph that contains the same five
parts in the third person.

## Thank-you / follow-up after a discovery conversation

- Quote one specific thing they said. Say how it changed your thinking.
- If they offered a name: ask for the intro, offer a forwardable note.
- If not: "Who else should I be talking to about this?"
- No pitch. No "let me know if you'd like to see what we're building."

## Confirmation after a commitment

Restate the commitment with the date, what you'll bring, and who else they said should be
there. Send the same day.

## Zombie-lead revival (one attempt only)

One concrete ask with a cost attached ("run it on one real task next week, I'll set it
up"). Say a "no" is genuinely useful. If they don't answer, archive them.

## Output

Give the message, then two or three bullets: which of the five parts is weakest and why,
and what real detail the user could add to strengthen it. If the user gave a draft, mark
every sentence that pitches, flatters generically, or asks for an opinion, and rewrite.
