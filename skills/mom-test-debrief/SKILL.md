# Mom Test — Debrief

Input: notes, transcript, or summary of one or more customer conversations. Optionally the
founder's assumptions. Be blunt; the founder needs the fluff they counted as evidence
pointed out.

## 1. Facts vs. interpretations

Two lists. **Facts** are only things the customer said or did, as close to verbatim as
possible. **Interpretations** are the founder's (or your) guesses about what they mean.
If the notes only contain interpretations ("they're interested"), say so.

Tag each fact: ⚡ pain · 🎯 goal · ▢ obstacle/constraint · ⤴ workaround · $ money ·
⏱ time/frequency · ☺ named person · ☐ feature request (+ its "why") · 😀😠 emotion.

## 2. Evidence ladder

Place every customer statement on a rung. Then report: **count at rung 8+** and **highest
rung reached**.

```
 1 compliment            8 specific past incident      16 time committed to you (dated)
 2 opinion               9 emotional recounting         17 intro to decision-maker
 3 intention "I would"  10 named consequence            18 resources committed
 4 hypothetical "$X"    11 specific objection           19 money paid to you
 5 feature request      12 active search
 6 generic complaint    13 workaround built
 7 prompted agreement   14 time invested in problem
                        15 money spent on problem
```
Rungs 1–7: no evidence. 8–15: the problem is real. 16–19: the solution is wanted.
If everything is ≤7, say plainly: this conversation produced no evidence.

## 3. Scorecard (1 / 3 / 5 each, total /75)

| # | Category | 1 | 5 |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Talk ratio | founder talked most | customer talked 70%+ |
| 2 | Pitching | pitched early | no pitch, or flipped only after discovery was complete |
| 3 | Behavior questions | mostly opinions/hypotheticals | all about past/present behavior |
| 4 | Leading | founder named the problem, customer agreed | customer named problems unprompted |
| 5 | Current solutions | not asked | what, cost, what's wrong, why not switched |
| 6 | Costs | no figures | time, money, and consequence figures |
| 7 | Frequency | "often" | a countable number plus last instances |
| 8 | Urgency | not asked | trigger/deadline known, or its absence |
| 9 | Specific examples | none | two or more dated incidents |
| 10 | What they've tried | not asked | history of attempts and why each failed |
| 11 | Facts vs opinions | blur | every fact backed by a quote |
| 12 | Surprise | nothing new | something changed an assumption |
| 13 | Scary question | avoided | asked early, real answer |
| 14 | Compliments / fluff | logged as positive | deflected and anchored every one |
| 15 | Next step | "keep in touch" | commitment with a date, or a clear no, plus a new name |

Score only what the notes support; if a category can't be judged, say "unknown" and tell
the founder to capture it next time. Give the total, the two lowest categories, and one
sentence on what to do differently.

Interpretation: 15–30 no evidence · 31–45 facts but can't act on them · 46–60 solid
discovery · 61–75 expert (verify you're not grading generously).

## 4. Drift log

Count: compliments received, times the founder pitched or defended, "would/could/think"
questions asked, generics accepted without anchoring. Quote the worst one.

## 5. Assumptions

If the founder listed assumptions, mark each **SUPPORTED / WEAKENED / KILLED / UNTOUCHED**
with the fact that did it. If none were listed, infer the three the founder seems to be
holding and mark those. An assumption that nothing touched means the question wasn't asked.

## 6. Meeting result (solution stage only)

Commitment or advancement obtained? Name it (time / reputation / money, with date). If
none: **FAILED**. Say so. "Went well" is not a result.

## 7. Next steps

- Names to follow up (within 24h).
- The single most uncertain assumption, and who to talk to next to test it.
- A follow-up message draft: thanks + one specific quote + intro request or commitment
  confirmation. Under 80 words.

## Batch mode (2+ conversations)

Add a table: person · segment attributes (role, size, tools, stage) · top problem named
unprompted · highest rung. Then answer: are top problems consistent? If not, which
attribute splits them? Propose the narrower segment in one sentence. If three or more
people in one segment gave the same answer unprompted with recent instances, say the
problem is real for that segment and that discovery there can move to a commitment ask.
