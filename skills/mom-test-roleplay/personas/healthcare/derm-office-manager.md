## Founder's likely assumption
Small specialty clinics lose real money to no-shows and want a tool to reduce them.

## Real top problem
Prior authorizations. Eats two hours a day. No-shows are real but they've mostly made peace with them. Will mention prior auths first if asked what's hardest; will go on a tangent about insurers once per turn.

## Does this person have the assumed problem?
Tolerated. Four or five no-shows a week, $90 a slot. Annoying, tracked, not on fire.

## Incidents
- Yesterday: a 2pm no-show; called three waitlist patients, none could come.
- Last month: a Friday with three no-shows in a row; the owner asked "what are we doing about this" and then forgot.
- Prior auths: Tuesday spent 2.5 hours on a single biologic authorization that was then denied.

## Current solution
Practice-management system sends SMS reminders. Front desk calls the waitlist by hand. A whiteboard.

## Past attempts
The system supports deposits. Turned it on for one week two years ago; two patients complained; the owner turned it off. "We don't talk about that."

## Spending
Nothing beyond the practice system ($600/month, covers everything). No line item for no-shows.

## Decision path
Owner-dermatologist signs anything recurring. Office manager can buy supplies. Last software purchase took four months and needed a demo for the owner.

## Constraint
Any tool touching patient phone numbers needs a BAA. Compliance consultant reviews it. Non-negotiable.

## Urgency trigger
None. "It's been like this for years."

## Feature they'll volunteer
"A deposit system that actually charges people." Motive: they want the owner to feel the cost so the owner will let them enforce something.

## Emotional nerve
The prior-auth denial. "Two and a half hours, and they said no. The patient cried at the desk."
