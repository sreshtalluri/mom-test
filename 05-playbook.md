# Part 8 — Founder Conversation Playbook

**[Synthesis throughout, built on the book's prep/run/review process from Chapter 8 and the question rules from Chapters 1–5.]**

## 8.1 Before the conversation

### What you should know
- **Who they are.** Role, company size, tools they likely use, anything public. Don't spend conversation time on what LinkedIn could tell you.
- **Which segment they belong to.** So you know whether their answers should be compared with the last five people or start a new bucket.
- **What you already believe.** Written down. If you can't state the belief, you can't tell when it's been overturned.
- **Your three big questions.** The three things that, if answered, would most change what you do next.
- **Which one scares you.** Ask it early.

### What to prepare
- A broad opener that doesn't mention your problem. ("What's the hardest part of…")
- Three big questions, phrased about their past.
- Your deflection reflexes: compliment → their behavior; fluff → "when did that last happen?"; idea → "why?"
- If solution-stage: the specific commitment you'll ask for.
- A notes template open (Part 7).

### What NOT to prepare
- A pitch.
- A deck or demo, unless you're explicitly past discovery.
- A 15-question script. Three questions and curiosity beat fifteen questions and a clipboard.
- A definition of "success" that involves them liking anything.

### Assumptions to name
Write each as a falsifiable sentence: *"Clinic managers lose at least $300/week to no-shows."* Not *"no-shows are a problem."* Then write what answer would kill it.

### Outcomes you're looking for
- Discovery stage: at least one surprise, one specific incident, one cost figure, one current-solution fact, one name for follow-up.
- Solution stage: a commitment of time, reputation, or money, or a clear no.

## 8.2 During the conversation

### How to open
Casual. State a real, small purpose and hand them the floor.
> "Thanks. I'm trying to understand how [role] deals with [broad area], and I'd rather hear it from you than guess. Can you tell me what a normal [week/close/project] looks like?"

### How to establish context
One or two fact questions, fast: team size, tools, how long they've been doing this. Enough to interpret what follows. Then stop; context isn't the point.

### How to get them talking
- Ask about a recent, concrete event. Stories are easier to tell than opinions.
- Be quiet after the question. Count to three.
- Reflect their words. "Midnight?" "Ripped it out?"
- Show interest, not agreement. "Huh, why?" beats "totally."

### Broad to specific
```
"What's the hardest part of X?"          ← let them rank
      ↓ (they name something)
"When did that last happen?"             ← anchor to instance
      ↓
"Walk me through it."                    ← get the story
      ↓
"What did that cost / what happened?"    ← get consequences
      ↓
"What do you do about it now?"           ← get current solution
      ↓
"What have you tried?"                   ← get history of action
```
Don't jump to your assumed problem. If they never raise it, ask about it last, and note that it wasn't top of mind.

### How to dig deeper
The five-deep rule: for anything interesting, ask "why" or "and then what" until you hit money, time, or people. Most founders stop at two.

### How to follow interesting threads
When they say something surprising, drop your script. Say "wait, go back to that." The script will still be there. The surprise won't.

### How to avoid pitching
- Keep your idea in a sentence you've decided not to say until the flip.
- When you feel the urge, ask a question instead. Any question.
- If you slip: "Sorry, got carried away. You were saying…"

### How to handle compliments
"Thanks." Then immediately a behavioral question. Never build on it. Mark it in notes as a drift signal.

### How to handle vague answers
| They say | You say |
|---|---|
| "It depends." | "On what? Give me the most common case." |
| "Sometimes." | "When was the last time?" |
| "It's fine." | "What's the part that's least fine?" |
| "We manage." | "How? Walk me through the managing." |
| "All the time." | "How many times last week?" |

### How to handle "I'd definitely use that"
Don't argue, don't celebrate. Redirect to the past and to cost:
> "That's good to hear. What are you using now? … What would you have to stop doing to switch?"
If solution-stage, convert it to a commitment immediately: "Great. Can we book the setup for Tuesday?"

### How to handle objections
Welcome them. They're the strongest sign of serious consideration you'll get before commitment.
> "That's exactly what I need to know. Say more. What would it need to do?"
Never defend. Write it down verbatim.

### How to know when to stop asking
- Your three questions have specific answers.
- You've had at least one surprise, or you've confirmed there's nothing surprising here.
- They're repeating themselves.
- You've hit the time you asked for. Respect it; ask for more if it's going well.

Close with: "Who else should I talk to?" and "Is there anything I should have asked?"

## 8.3 After the conversation

### What to record (within 15 minutes)
- Exact quotes (at least three).
- Every number: hours, dollars, counts, dates.
- Current solution and its cost.
- Named people and roles.
- Surprises.
- Commitments made, with dates.
- Your drift moments (compliments received, times you pitched).

### How to evaluate
Place each item on the Signal Strength Ladder (Part 5). Count how many are rung 8 or above. If zero, the conversation produced no evidence.

### Facts vs. interpretations
Two columns. Left: what they said or did, verbatim. Right: what you think it means. The team can argue about the right column. Nobody argues about the left.

### How to update assumptions
For each written assumption: **supported**, **weakened**, **killed**, or **untouched**. Note which conversation did it. If a conversation touched no assumptions, either the assumptions are wrong or the conversation was chatting.

### What to do next
- Follow up every name within 24 hours.
- Send a thank-you that references one specific thing they said.
- If they committed, confirm the date in writing the same day.
- Pick the next three people based on what would test the most-uncertain assumption.

# Part 9 — Conversation Decision Tree

**[Synthesis]** Keep this open. Find the closest thing they just said, and take the branch.

```
START: "What's the hardest part of [their domain]?"
│
├─ They name YOUR problem ──────────────► go to PROBLEM NAMED
├─ They name a DIFFERENT problem ───────► follow it (PROBLEM NAMED), come back to yours last
└─ "Nothing really / it's all fine" ────► "What took the most time last week?" then PROBLEM NAMED
                                           (if still nothing → note: no pain in this segment)

PROBLEM NAMED: "When did that last happen?"
│
├─ Specific incident with details ──────► go to INCIDENT
├─ "All the time / always" ─────────────► "Tell me about the most recent one." → INCIDENT
└─ Can't recall one ────────────────────► note: low salience. "Has it ever been bad?" 
                                           still nothing → problem doesn't matter to them. Move on.

INCIDENT: "Walk me through it. What happened?"
│
└─ (listen for time, money, people, emotion) ──► "What did that cost you?"
   │
   ├─ Time figure ──────────────────────► "How often?" → FREQUENCY
   ├─ Money figure ─────────────────────► "Where did that money come from?" → DECISION
   ├─ Emotional but no figure ──────────► "And then what happened?" (repeat until figure or dead end)
   └─ "Not much, honestly" ─────────────► note: low severity. "Where does it rank?" → PRIORITY

FREQUENCY: "How many times last month?"
│
├─ Countable, recurring ────────────────► "What do you do about it now?" → CURRENT SOLUTION
├─ Rare but severe ─────────────────────► "What did the worst one cost?" → back to INCIDENT
└─ Vague ("often") ─────────────────────► "Can you think of the last three?" → if no → low salience

CURRENT SOLUTION: "How do you handle it today?"
│
├─ Named tool / vendor ─────────────────► "What do you pay? What's wrong with it? Why not switch?" → SPENDING
├─ Manual workaround ───────────────────► "Show me. How long does it take? Who does it?" → SPENDING
├─ Person hired / assigned ─────────────► "What's their time on this worth?" → SPENDING
└─ "Nothing" ───────────────────────────► "Have you looked?" 
                                            yes → "What stopped you?" → CONSTRAINTS
                                            no  → problem isn't important enough. Note it.

SPENDING: "Have you ever paid for anything to fix this?"
│
├─ Yes, with a number ──────────────────► "Was it worth it? Would you spend it again?" → DECISION
├─ Tried and abandoned ─────────────────► "Why did it fail?" → CONSTRAINTS (this is gold)
└─ Never ───────────────────────────────► "What would have to happen for you to?" → URGENCY

DECISION: "Who decided on that? Who'd decide on a fix?"
│
├─ Them ────────────────────────────────► "What's your threshold to not think about it?" → URGENCY
├─ Someone else, named ─────────────────► "Could you introduce me?" (commitment ask) → URGENCY
└─ "Not sure / committee" ──────────────► "How did the last purchase in this area go?" → map it

CONSTRAINTS: "What would stop something from working here?"
│
└─ Record verbatim. Ask "Is that a hard no or a preference?" → URGENCY

URGENCY: "What's making this matter now, if anything?"
│
├─ Trigger event / deadline ────────────► strong. → PRIORITY
├─ "It's always been like this" ────────► chronic, tolerated. → PRIORITY (expect low rank)
└─ "We're actively looking" ────────────► "What have you looked at?" → very strong → PRIORITY

PRIORITY: "Where does this rank against everything else you're dealing with?"
│
├─ Top 3 ───────────────────────────────► discovery complete for this person. → FLIP? 
├─ Mid-list ────────────────────────────► "What's ahead of it?" (that may be your real problem)
└─ Bottom ──────────────────────────────► note it. "Who has this worse?" → WHO ELSE

FLIP? (only if every box above is filled AND you're solution-stage)
│
├─ Yes ──► one-sentence solution tied to their words → "What would make this not work?" → COMMIT
└─ No ───► WHO ELSE

COMMIT: ask for time / intro / money, with a date
│
├─ Yes, specific ───────────────────────► confirm in writing today. SUCCESS.
├─ Hesitation / "let me think" ─────────► "What would you need to see?" → record. Not yet.
└─ No ──────────────────────────────────► "What's the blocker?" → record. Real answer obtained.

WHO ELSE: "Who else should I talk to? Anything I should have asked?"
END.
```

### Compact version for a sticky note
```
Problem? → When last? → Walk me through → Cost? → How often?
→ Doing now? → Tried / paid? → Who decides? → What blocks? → Why now?
→ Rank? → [flip only if all filled] → Ask for commitment → Who else?
```

# Part 10 — Follow-Up Question Engine

## 10.1 The principle

**[Synthesis]** Every customer statement contains a gap. The best follow-up is the one that fills the gap you most need filled right now. So the engine has two steps: **classify the statement**, then **pick the follow-up by what you're missing.**

## 10.2 Classify the statement

| Statement type | Marker | Default move |
|---|---|---|
| **Generic claim** | always, never, usually, everyone | Anchor to instance |
| **Future / hypothetical** | would, could, might, will | Redirect to past |
| **Opinion** | I think, I feel, it seems | Ask for the experience behind it |
| **Compliment** | cool, great, love, smart | Deflect to behavior |
| **Feature request** | you should, it needs, add | Dig for motive |
| **Specific incident** | date, number, name | Expand and cost it |
| **Emotion** | tone, sigh, "honestly" | Open the door: "say more" |
| **Constraint** | can't, won't, never allowed | Verify hardness |
| **Objection** | but, what about | Welcome and detail |
| **Name / role** | a person, a team | Ask for intro or role in decision |

## 10.3 Pick the follow-up by what you're missing

Take any statement and check which of these you still don't have. Ask for the most important missing one.

| Missing | Follow-up |
|---|---|
| **Instance** | "When did that last happen?" |
| **Story** | "Walk me through it." |
| **Frequency** | "How many times last month?" |
| **Time cost** | "How long did that take?" |
| **Money cost** | "What did that cost? What do you pay now?" |
| **Consequence** | "What happened as a result?" |
| **Action taken** | "What have you tried?" |
| **Why it failed** | "Why didn't that work?" |
| **Current solution** | "How are you handling it today?" |
| **Owner** | "Who deals with it? Who decides?" |
| **Urgency** | "What's making this matter now?" |
| **Priority** | "Where does it rank?" |
| **Motive (for ideas)** | "Why do you want that? What would it let you do?" |
| **Hardness (for constraints)** | "Is that a hard rule or a preference?" |
| **Emotion source** | "What's behind that?" |

## 10.4 Worked example

**Customer:** "We've struggled with this for years."

Classification: generic claim + mild emotion. Missing: everything.

| If you most need… | Ask |
|---|---|
| To know if it's real | "When did it last bite you?" |
| To know how bad | "What's the worst it's gotten?" |
| To know if they've acted | "What have you tried over those years?" |
| To know why it's unsolved | "Why hasn't anything stuck?" |
| To know the cost | "What's it costing you in a typical month?" |
| To know who owns it | "Who's usually the one dealing with it?" |
| To know urgency | "Why is it still on your list after years? What's changed?" |
| To know spending | "Have you ever paid for anything to fix it?" |

**Choosing the best one:** early in discovery, choose "when did it last bite you?" because without an instance, nothing else is verifiable. Later, when you've confirmed the problem is real across several people, choose "why hasn't anything stuck?" because that reveals the constraints that will kill or shape your product. At solution stage, choose "what's changed?" because urgency is what converts.

## 10.5 More worked statements

| Customer says | Best early follow-up | Best late follow-up |
|---|---|---|
| "Our team wastes so much time on this." | "How much time, last week?" | "Who owns fixing it?" |
| "I'd love something that did X." | "How do you do X today?" | "Would you try a rough version next week?" |
| "We looked at a tool for that once." | "Which one? What happened?" | "What would've made you buy it?" |
| "That's not really my area." | "Whose is it?" | "Could you introduce me?" |
| "It's not a huge deal." | "Where does it rank?" | (move on; this person isn't your customer) |
| "The real problem is actually Y." | "Tell me about the last time Y happened." | "Is Y bigger than X for you?" |
| "IT would never let us." | "Is that a hard rule? What's the last thing they blocked?" | "Who at IT would need to say yes?" |
| "We just hired someone for that." | "What do they do all day?" | "What does that role cost?" |

## 10.6 A rule for choosing under pressure

**[Synthesis]** If you can't decide in two seconds, ask **"When did that last happen?"** It's never wrong, it always moves toward the past, and it buys you time to think.

# Part 11 — Founder Customer Discovery OS

**[Synthesis]** The whole book compressed into an operating system with eleven modules.

### 1. Mindset
- Detective, not salesperson. Learning, not validating.
- Your idea is a hypothesis. Their past is the evidence.
- Bad news is progress. Surprises are the point. Comfort is the warning sign.
- You are not there to be right. You're there to stop being wrong sooner.

### 2. Objectives
Per conversation: three big questions, one scary one, one surprise, one name.
Per batch: one assumption confirmed or killed.
Per segment: consistent top problem across 5+ people, or a new slice.
Per stage: discovery until the problem is understood; validation until commitments arrive.

### 3. Questioning
- About them. About the past. Answerable with a fact or story.
- Broad first ("hardest part of…"), then anchor ("when last?"), then expand ("walk me through"), then cost, then current solution, then decision, then urgency.
- No would/could/might/like/think.
- One scary question, early.

### 4. Listening
- Silence after questions. Reflect last words. Follow tangents.
- Track: specifics, money, time, workarounds, emotion, urgency, constraints, decision path, objections, contradictions, absences.
- Alarm on: compliments, fluff, your own voice.

### 5. Evidence
- Signal Strength Ladder. Rungs 1–7: no evidence. 8–15: discovery evidence. 16–19: validation evidence.
- Past beats future. Cost beats words. Specific beats generic.
- Facts in one column, interpretations in another.

### 6. Validation
- Problem is real when: multiple people in one segment name it unprompted, describe specific recent instances, have spent time or money on it, and rank it high.
- Solution is wanted when: people give up time, reputation, or money for it.
- Nothing else counts.

### 7. Outreach
- Go where they are. Host if you can. Ask everyone for the next name.
- Frame the ask: vision, not selling, specific weakness, their expertise, small ask.
- Casual beats formal. Five minutes beats thirty.

### 8. Discovery
- Open broad. Anchor. Expand. Cost. Solution. Decision. Urgency. Rank.
- Deflect, anchor, dig. Don't pitch. Don't defend.
- Close: who else, what did I miss.

### 9. Follow-Up
- Every name within 24h. Thank-you with a specific reference.
- Commitments confirmed in writing same day.
- Zombie leads get a concrete ask; if refused, they're archived.

### 10. Documentation
- Per conversation: quotes, numbers, solution, people, surprises, commitments, drift.
- Assumption tracker: belief, status, evidence, what would change it.
- Weekly review with whole team. Raw notes shared, not just summaries.

### 11. Decision Making
- Build for the segment whose top problem you've heard five times.
- Price against the cost of the current solution, not against a guess.
- Sell to the person who decided on the last purchase, not the one who complained.
- Kill assumptions that three conversations contradicted, even if you love them.
- When signals conflict, narrow the segment before adding features.
- When nothing surprises you anymore, stop discovering and start asking for commitments.
